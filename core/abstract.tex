\section*{Abstract}
To improve the safety and reliability of Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) in chaotic traffic environments, we are developing a Synthetic Dataset (SD) for Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) focusing on the difficulty of traffic and human behaviors, in order to enhance the safety and reliability of AVs in chaotic traffic scenarios. The main purpose of our work is to create high quality and diverse datasets with advanced simulation. These datasets enable AVs to be well suited for the common road networks found in South Asia, which are normally unstructured. At present, most perception systems are trained on roads from the western part of the world, which are more structured. This leads to these systems breaking down at every intersection at which rickshaws cross lanes, pedestrians walk between moving vehicles, and rickshaws (which do not exist in any western dataset) are found. If a Nosimon were coming down a street in front of a bus, and there was a pedestrian running down the street in the middle of the street, that's not the kind of thing you would want to have a camera crew go and film. Hence, we created a "data factory" based on a simulation, which was capable of generating any visual class or behaviour edge case that we required, in large volume, without risking anyone's life. We want the autonomy that learns from this data not only to perceive the road accurately, but to understand the social logic of South Indian streets, where the signals are non-autonomous and the flow is not the one in Western cities, and where the street is the one that is often unpredictable, and one that no Western benchmark has tried to seriously understand.
